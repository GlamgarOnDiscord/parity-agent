# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The demo already has its moment: a one-cent rounding difference on a tax-on-tax total, found in an old Windows app. It is concrete, it can be checked, and anyone who has done accounting will feel it. In the Product lens, a single moment like this is worth more than a feature list.
- The split between AI and determinism is the right shape. AI captures and explains. The replay and the comparison are plain code. That is the part nobody sees, and it is the part that earns trust.
- The human validates rules in plain language. That keeps the expert in charge of the one judgment the machine should not make alone.
- The narrow original version (old business app, rebuilt as a web app, parity proven) fits in one sentence. The pitched version does not.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** "Any change to any software, for as many people as possible" is three products for three buyers: developers using AI agents, IT service providers, and non-technical owners. Each one has a different first five minutes. Trying to serve all three in 9 days means none of them gets a finished experience, and the video shows a diagram, not a product.
2. **Too many seams.** The pipeline is video, transcription, segmentation, autonomous exploration, rule extraction, human validation, replay, diff and explanation. That is about eight handoffs between models, sandboxes and people, and none of it is built yet. The step I trust least is matching "every field" between an old Windows UI and a new web UI with a different layout. If that mapping is unreliable, the proof is not a proof.
3. **The first five minutes ask a lot.** The user records, narrates, waits for exploration, then reviews a list of rules before they get any value. Nothing is known about how long each step takes or how many extracted rules are wrong. If the review is long, the expert drops out.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is it in one sentence, for one buyer?
- Walk me through the 3-minute video second by second. Which seconds are live and which are mocked?
- How are fields matched across two UIs that look nothing alike: by position, by label, by value, or by hand?
- Of the 15 rules you observed by hand, how many does the pipeline extract on its own, and how many does it get wrong? (Open question: no number exists.)
- If Muse Spark exploration fails or runs too long, does the demo still work from the recording alone?
- What would you cut first if day 6 goes badly?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Pitch and build one thing: proving that a rebuilt legacy business app does the same thing as the old one. Keep "any software" to one closing line about where it could go.
- Run one scenario from start to end live, on Virtual Salesman 3, with the one-cent difference found, explained and backed by evidence on screen. Breadth comes after.
- Freeze a cut list by day 3. Autonomous exploration and FrontAccounting are optional and get dropped if they put the main path at risk.
- Treat the rule-review screen and the diff screen as the product. Give them real craft and keep everything else plain.
