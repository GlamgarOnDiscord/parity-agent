# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The engineering question.** Capturing behaviour from a running binary with no source, under Wine, then replaying it deterministically, is a step change and not a small improvement over "read the old code and hope". The one-cent tax-on-tax rounding gap is the kind of thing manual review misses. It is also exactly what the technical depth and demo criteria reward.
- **Timing.** AI agents are writing code that nobody reviews line by line, review costs are rising, Windows 10 is out of support, and veteran staff are retiring. "Why now" has a real answer.
- **A possible secret.** If the brief is right that the hard part is undocumented edge cases, then the asset is the validated proof, not the AI. That framing is uncommon.
- **Cost structure.** AI runs once at capture and replay is deterministic, so the cost of each verification can approach zero. Over time that could become an economy of scale.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No small market to own.** "As many people as possible" and "any change to any software" put it up against record-and-replay, snapshot and regression testing tools, plus the AI agent vendors themselves. Nobody has made a competitor list. Starting broad means starting with no share.
2. **No durability.** It is open source, free, and built on capabilities (computer use, segmentation, transcription) that every model provider is getting better at. A well-funded copycat, or an agent platform shipping "verify against old behaviour" as a built-in feature, could match the demo by month six. No network effect, brand or proprietary data exists yet.
3. **No distribution.** One solo student, a price of zero, and no channel. How the first 100 users arrive is unanswered, and so is what each one costs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which narrow segment could Parity dominate first: IT service providers migrating legacy Windows business apps, a single vertical (accounting, point of sale), or something else? How many such firms exist? (Open question: no market size yet.)
- Who sells parity or behaviour-verification within reach of that buyer today?
- What accumulates with use that a copycat cannot clone: a library of validated rule proofs, a proof format others adopt, or audit-grade evidence that insurers or regulators accept?
- How do the first 100 users find it, and at what acquisition cost? (Open question.)
- Will the full pipeline work end to end on one laptop in 9 days, or only part of it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Hackathon:** pitch the legacy rebuild as the beachhead, not as a narrowed-down version. Show one complete loop on Virtual Salesman 3 (capture, rules validated, rebuild, replay, cent-level difference explained). Present "any software" as the expansion path, not the claim.
2. **After:** name one segment and talk to 10 buyers in it before widening.
3. **Pick a moat to build.** The most likely candidate is an open, versioned proof format plus a shared corpus of validated rules, so that each proof makes the next capture cheaper.
4. **Name one channel**, with an estimated cost per user, before deciding on any paid tier.
